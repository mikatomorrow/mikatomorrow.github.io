2.4 GHz Cascode LNA Design & Optimization

Personal Project  |  Spring 2025  |  130 nm CMOS  |  Cadence Virtuoso / Spectre

Overview

Designed, simulated, and optimized a differential cascode Low Noise Amplifier (LNA) in 130 nm CMOS targeting the 2.4 GHz ISM band. The LNA is the first active stage in a receiver chain, and its noise figure and gain directly determine the cascaded noise performance of the entire system. The design prioritizes low noise figure while maintaining adequate input matching and gain.

The topology uses inductive input matching to achieve a 50Ω input impedance at 2.4 GHz, a differential cascode input pair for gain and noise performance, and an LC resonant tank at the output tuned to 2.4 GHz to filter out-of-band noise.

Architecture

  • Input matching (white): Inductive network creating 50Ω match and tuning input resonance to 2.4 GHz
  • Input transistors M1/M2 (pink): Differential pair providing voltage gain (Aᵥ = gₘ · R); dominant noise contributors — sized for minimum noise figure. W/L = 400µm / 0.13µm
  • Cascode devices M3/M4 (green): Improve reverse isolation (reduce S₁₂ leakage) and boost gain; W/L = 400µm / 0.30µm
  • Output LC tank (green): Resonant at 2.4 GHz to filter high-frequency noise and present a high impedance load at the target frequency
